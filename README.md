# STRSearch — The Client Journey

A visually led leadership presentation that shows how STRSearch turns paying clients into property owners.

**Live:** https://ifrahamstr.github.io/STRSearchExecutiveReport/ opens the default version. Two reporting versions are published (see [`VERSIONS.md`](VERSIONS.md)):
- **16 Nov 2026 · Updated reporting (default):** https://ifrahamstr.github.io/STRSearchExecutiveReport/2026-11-16/ · Supabase reporting layer · data as of 2026-11-16.
- **12 Nov 2026 · Original reporting (archived, unchanged):** https://ifrahamstr.github.io/STRSearchExecutiveReport/2026-11-12/ · DIM extracts · data as of 2026-11-11.

The date menu in the header switches version and keeps the chapter. Links to the Business Data Explorer open the same version there.

**Rebuild:** `python pipeline/build.py --cache` (16 Nov, imports the Explorer's 16 Nov foundation and checks it matches the published Explorer). `python versions/2026-11-12/pipeline/build.py --cache` reproduces the frozen 12 Nov data into `versions/2026-11-12/rebuild/`.

**Using this repo with an AI assistant?** Start with [`LLM_CONTEXT.md`](LLM_CONTEXT.md). Every figure is in `docs/<version>/data/story-data.json`.

## 16 Nov 2026 headline figures
- Durations (won engagements with a confirmed Jira start, n = 825): median 37.5 days to Under Contract (mean 56.17, n = 492), median 73.0 to CLOSED (mean 92.82, n = 438); Kaplan–Meier 76 / 117 days.

## Cash forecast tab (16 Nov 2026 only)
`docs/2026-11-16/cash.html` (header tab "Cash forecast") forecasts net future cash: second Split Pay installments + affiliate revenue − expected refunds. Built by `pipeline/chapters/cash.py` from the reporting layer's `client_cash_forecast`, the 12 Nov Jira / Pipedrive / affiliate-tracking extracts and both onboarding-form sheets; only aggregates are published.
- Reconciliation: all 876 won engagements sit in exactly one group and add up to the view's outstanding balance. The 47 won deals without a Jira match were resolved by e-mail, Jira key, onboarding forms and brokerage/lender records: 6 own Jira issue (5 matched by e-mail, 1 second deal whose own Jira issue is named by the Pipedrive deal and Jira's deal-id field), 2 closing record, 7 second deals under review (same client, won date and value as another deal, won within seconds or named by the same Jira key in Pipedrive, recorded as "Deal 2"; no engagement of their own, so they stay excluded and are flagged), 9 repeat purchases without their own issue, 23 with no record. Matching client, date and value is not treated as proof of a duplicate: two-search purchases look identical (13 of the 14 same-client wins within a minute are named as a second deal, and in 6 both deals have their own Jira issue). No confirmed duplicates. One of the seven second deals is matched only by the Jira key typed in Pipedrive (it has no e-mail match); the rule checks that key after the e-mail and record checks, so it never overrides closing evidence. Of 22 post-close records without a closing date, 3 closings were found in affiliate records; the rest are shown as unresolved.
- Model: under-contract clients close with the historical probability for their elapsed time (UC → CLOSED), timed by the remaining historical durations; clients without a property follow the historical close / refund / pause shares for their stage and, since Batch 4D, their own historical timing (see the timing bullet below); paused clients who resume or are refunded spread around the "Expected days until collection" slider (37 days earliest to an adjustable maximum of 77) with the historical UC → CLOSED variation (a modeling assumption, not validated against history; the page says so); every approved refund returns 100% of the upfront payment (fixed rule, no slider); paused / additional-service clients have three outcomes that always add up to 100% (default since Batch 4D: 10% resume and close / 40% full refund / 50% remain paused; before: 50 / 50 / 0): resume and close pays the second installment and earns affiliate fees, full refund returns the upfront payment and the second installment is never collected, remain paused creates no cash in or out within the forecast period; clients marked Refunded in Jira are historical refunds, already paid back and not in the forecast; affiliates use each client's recorded partner choice and the conversion sliders only where none is recorded. The published timing weights (rounded to 6 decimals, summing to 0.999999) are scaled to sum to exactly 1 in the browser, so every total and split reconciles to the cent.
- Defaults (after Batch 4D): $1,360,698.01 expected service collections + $896,752.34 affiliate − $461,303.78 projected refunds = $1,796,146.57 estimated future net cash (about $1.36M + $0.90M − $0.46M = $1.80M). The whole change from 4C comes from the new paused defaults (10 / 40 / 50); the 4D timing change moves cash between months only. After 4C (paused 50 / 50 / 0): $1,731,858.01 + $1,186,516.34 − $558,673.78 = $2,359,700.57; before 4C: $1,743,396.90 + $1,200,075.60 − $638,438.69 = $2,305,033.81. $0.41M of unresolved balances ($358K) and second deals under review ($55K) is shown but not forecast. Affiliate payout timing moves cash by 31 Dec from $1.13M (counted at closing) to $0.73M with no change to any total.
- Presentation (`docs/2026-11-16/assets/js/cash.js`, `assets/css/cash.css`): client landscape; KPIs (headline = "Estimated future net cash", under the selected assumptions); a left-to-right waterfall from the balance on contracts to net cash (indigo totals, green adds, red reduces; shaded groups "Not future cash" and "Not expected to pay"; "Cancelled by past refunds" is a balance that will never be billed, not a cash outflow; "Assumed collected at closing" is a balance treated as collected under the Split Pay rule); **monthly cash flow directly after the waterfall**; a Paused financial exposure section (installments still owed, upfront paid / refundable, potential affiliate revenue, the three outcomes with their cash effect and the range); affiliate revenue by partner (recorded partner choices vs modeled conversions, with modeled payout dates); a timing comparison ("Earlier collections / Your timing / Later collections": same total, different timing); refunds by source. The five chart views have a Chart / Table switch with sortable tables that follow the controls. Refunds by source and affiliate revenue by partner are display-only splits of the same model flows. The page uses a 1280px stage; desktop is the supported layout.
- Labels: every figure is Recorded (source records), Inferred (history or matching records) or Scenario (set by the controls); chips with distinct shapes (solid, half, dashed) so colour is never the only cue.
- Progressive disclosure: collapsed-by-default expandable explanations (`details[data-x]`, nested `details[data-n]` rows) for the headline, every waterfall step, each month of the monthly chart, the paused exposure and its three outcomes, each affiliate partner, refunds by source, the unresolved records and each control. Their text is rebuilt from the same model on every change (`XR` builders in cash.js), only while open, and rows the reader opened stay open. A timing note under the monthly chart states that the month-by-month pattern (January 2027 in particular) follows the expected-days setting and is not validated against history.
- Simulation switch (Refunds card): "Include projected refunds from clients still searching" (default ON = the historical refund rate for clients without a property). OFF removes only that refund outflow (about $69K at the defaults; $147K before Batch 4C); their collections, closing chances and affiliate revenue are unchanged, and paused / under-contract refunds are untouched. It is a display-side simulation setting; no historical data changes.
- Historical comparison groups (Batch 4C, `cash.history()`): a past client counts as still searching at day N only until the first exit event, the earliest of the Under Contract, Passed Inspection or closing date (53 of 182 mature closers have no Under Contract date: they leave at the inspection or closing date; none is invented), the refund date or the pause-entry date (12 Nov extract, latest status date), and only while observed for longer than N days. Under-contract failures leave the contract group at the refund, pause-entry or next-status date (all 20 dated). Outcomes stay mutually exclusive (closed > refunded > paused > still searching); records with an unknown outcome stay out, as before. When fewer than 10 past clients remain at risk, the last day with 10 is used (still searching: beyond day 451, 5 current clients), never the whole cohort. Effect: still-searching close / refund / pause-or-searching 59.3% / 12.7% / 28.0% → 55.9% / 6.0% / 38.1%; under contract 87.5% → 90.6%, scheduled 91.8% → 93.4%. Curves and exit counts are published in `cash.history.curves` / `cash.history.exits`. Timing distributions are unchanged (Batch 4D).
- Timing for clients still searching (Batch 4D, `cash.history()` close_timing / refund_timing; `cash.noprop.timing`): each client's expected collection and affiliate closings (the 4C amounts, unchanged) are spread over the remaining days to closing (closing day − days already searching) of the past closers still in the same 4C comparison group at that day (182 mature closers; the closing date is the target, so the 53 without an Under Contract date count fully; no date is invented). Closings more than a year after the start (5) are weighted by 1 / share of the mature cohort observed that long (inverse-probability-of-censoring weights, 1.0 up to day 366, max 3.3). Fewer than 10 past closers in the group: the last day with 10 is used (day 227; 16 current clients). Refunds are timed separately: the remaining days of the 76 dated past refunds (outcome refunded) made after the client's current day (median 14 days after start); fewer than 10 later refunds: the last day with 10 (day 77; 44 clients). Recent starts cannot yet show long refund delays, so refunds may be timed slightly early. Published as day arrays (rounded) and scaled in the browser to the 4C totals to the cent. The horizon runs to the last flow (day 589, July 2028 with the realtor payout); monthly charts use two-line month labels beyond 12 months. Share of still-searching collections by 31 Dec / 31 Jan / 30 Jun 2027: 24% / 46% / 80% (old spread: 10% / 91% / 100%). The expected-days slider, presets and timing scenarios now move only paused clients' flows. Paused and under-contract timing are unchanged.
- Affiliate payout timing is the baseline (no switch): each partner's affiliate cash moves from its projected closing day to a modeled receipt day, the 15th of the payout month (modeled, not confirmed payment dates). Realtor is paid in the month after the closing's calendar quarter ends (Q4 2026 closings in January 2027); lender and STR Cribs in the month after closing (STR Cribs provisional). The horizon extends to the last payout, so totals per partner are unchanged. At the defaults, cash by 31 Dec is $0.73M; realtor Q4 2026 fees ($264K) land in January 2027 and Q1 2027 fees ($156K) in April 2027.
- Upcoming source change (not implemented here): estimated closing dates of Under Contract + 30 days for UC / Passed Inspection records without one. The pipeline treats any `closed_raw` on or before the reporting date as a closing (second installment collected; counted in the 30-day closings). 13 of today's 59 such records ($129K of second installments) would get an estimated date already in the past, and the other 46 ($452K) would become dated scheduled closings. The source should flag estimated dates (or keep them in a separate column), and the pipeline should use them only for timing, never as evidence of a closing. The payout schedules key off the projected closing day, so they work with either source.
- Story order: (1) client landscape, KPIs and the "How is this number built?" explanation, (2) waterfall, (3) monthly cash flow (simple view: service collections, affiliate revenue, future refunds, net; "Split by client group" shows the client groups), (4) paused clients, then supporting analysis: affiliate revenue, timing comparison, refunds by source, an expandable unresolved-records breakdown (second deals under review listed separately) and the won-deal reconciliation table.

## Data
- Every figure comes from the [Business Data Explorer](https://github.com/ifrahamSTR/STRSearchBusinessDataExplorer-Public). This repo imports its pipeline (`definitions.py`, `build.py`, `metrics.py`) and uses its headline metrics directly.
- The chapter modules only reshape those figures for the visuals (bin counts, curve points). There is no new analysis and no imputed dates.
- Chapter 3 uses the Explorer's monthly counts (new Jira issues by created date, signed clients by client start, first Under Contract / CLOSED dates), its onboarding-first funnel classes and its same-cohort view (Under Contract within 90 days). Each build checks that the three intake classes add up to the Explorer's monthly totals, that each monthly gap is the Explorer's signed starts minus its Under Contract events, and that cohort sizes match its monthly signed starts.
- Chapter 5 also reads the onboarding form (`Onboarding STR Search (Responses).xlsx`, sheet *Form Responses 2*) and matches it to Jira by hashed client e-mail (preferred or secondary answer vs Jira client and additional e-mails).
  - The column headed "Person ID" (AH) actually holds Pipedrive **deal** ids. Each build checks this against the e-mail matches, and the column is used only as a deal-id fallback.
  - An answer counts only if it was given before the client's first Under Contract date.
  - Each share uses only clients who have had time to reach that milestone (30+ or 75+ days on the clock).
  - It outputs only aggregates. No share rests on fewer than 5 clients, and suppressed counts are not published.
- `pipeline/build.py` checks the output against the Explorer's own metrics and stops on any mismatch.

```bash
python pipeline/build.py --cache        # defaults: ../STRSearch-Business-Data-Explorer and its default reports folder
```

**Adding a chapter:**
1. Add `pipeline/chapters/<name>.py` with `build(ctx) -> dict`, and register it in `CHAPTERS`.
2. Add `docs/assets/js/chapters/<name>.js` calling `S.chapter('<name>', { n, title, render })`.
3. Add one `<script>` tag in `docs/index.html`.

The journey rail and keyboard stepping pick the new chapter up automatically.
